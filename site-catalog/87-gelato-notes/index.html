Things to try

  1. Fruit and ice

    Sketch a frozen fruit dessert with a recorded fruit-to-liquid ratio and portion size. Compare sweetness after cooling without claiming a universal ideal flavor.

  2. Creamy balance

    Make a small recipe table that varies fat and sugar separately, noting how both can affect perceived creaminess. Follow ordinary food-hygiene rules throughout.

  3. A simple cone

    Draw a serving cone with balance, grip and melting time in mind. A neat silhouette cannot replace stable handling and a safe food-contact surface.

  4. Seasonal flavor

    Create a seasonal flavor map using locally available fruits and one contrasting ingredient each. Keep invented names separate from any claim of availability.

Fruit and ice

Start here: A frozen-fruit texture comparison

Frozen desserts depend on water, sugar and serving temperature. A notebook can compare texture without pretending to test commercial products.

  1. Use a known food-safe fruit puree in two small labelled containers with different but recorded proportions of puree and added water.

  2. Freeze under the same appliance settings and record whether large ice crystals or a softer texture appear after a safe serving interval.

What to notice: Mixture: Ingredients and proportions are recorded.; Freezer: Containers and placement are similar.; Texture: Observations are descriptive, not laboratory data.
