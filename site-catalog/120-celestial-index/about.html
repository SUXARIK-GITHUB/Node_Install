Method and limits

The following is an independent reading exercise, not a report of fieldwork.

The Moon is a useful starting point for a sky notebook because its appearance changes predictably but visibility depends on location and weather. A simple drawing can preserve a real observation without claiming professional precision.

Repeat later in the month. Compare shape, time of day and the position relative to the horizon, remembering that a single sketch cannot determine all orbital properties.

Never observe the Sun through an unfiltered telescope or binoculars. A static page cannot provide current rise times or local visibility.
