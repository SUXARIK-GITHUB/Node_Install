Questions

Answers on objects as evidence, typography in time and related reading from Form Museum.

Objects as evidence: What makes an object credible evidence?

Objects become evidence when their materials and context are recorded.

Typography in time: How can historical typography be compared?

Typography changes with available tools and reading habits. Compare specimens from similar periods to avoid turning one unusual example into a trend.

Material samples: What belongs on a material sample label?

Arrange a few safe material swatches by texture and process and give each a precise caption. Visitor access and preservation may require different handling.

What should I check in this material: Object?

Visible material and construction described. Example: Choose an object you own and record observable details: materials, dimensions, joints and signs of wear.

What should I check in this material: Source?

Every historical claim has a citation. Example: Write an eighty-word label that separates the visible features from claims requiring catalog research.

What should I check in this material: Unknowns?

Missing attribution is explicit. Example: Add a note identifying where provenance could be checked and mark any unknown author or date as unknown.

What are the limits of this exercise?

Do not reproduce protected catalog images without rights or invent a museum collection around an ordinary object.
