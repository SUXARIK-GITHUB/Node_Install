Steam and crust

Steam delays crust setting and allows the loaf to expand early.

Steam delays crust setting early in baking, while later drying helps browning. Use safe methods appropriate to your oven and its manufacturer instructions.

Compare the appearance of two baked crusts with recorded steam and heat settings. Treat browning as one clue, not the only proof of an evenly baked loaf.

What to look for: A hydration note for a small loaf

Hydration describes a ratio, but dough behavior also depends on flour, mixing, temperature and time. Compare two small doughs by changing one variable and noting elasticity rather than judging by the clock alone. Allow a loaf to cool before evaluating the crumb, and treat food-safety guidance as separate from a texture experiment.

Field guide

A hydration note for a small loaf

A useful baking log separates ingredient ratios from fermentation time. Small variations in flour and room temperature may change the dough.

Method

  1. Write a sample formula with 250 g flour and 160 g water; calculate hydration as water divided by flour mass.
  2. Prepare a second worksheet with a modestly different water amount, keeping yeast and salt notes separate rather than changing all variables.
  3. Record observations about stickiness, shape and proofing conditions without promising identical results in every kitchen.
