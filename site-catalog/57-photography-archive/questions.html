Questions

Answers on contact sheets, quiet portraits and related reading from Frame Archive.

Contact sheets: Why work with a contact sheet?

Contact sheets reveal a sequence that a single chosen photograph hides.

Quiet portraits: How do you make a portrait respectfully?

A portrait can feel quiet because the subject has room to look outside the frame. Consider whether the background gives context or distracts.

Light direction: What changes when light shifts direction?

Photograph the same harmless object under side and frontal light with unchanged framing. Describe shadow direction rather than attributing a mood as a fact.

What should I check in this material: Sequence?

All candidate frames remain traceable. Example: Choose six photographs of one ordinary subject and retain the unedited sequence and original timestamps in a private folder.

What should I check in this material: Technical review?

Focus and exposure judged separately from taste. Example: Create thumbnails in capture order and mark focus, exposure and composition problems without deleting the rejected frames.

What should I check in this material: Caption?

Location and people only when permission allows. Example: Select one frame and write a caption explaining the visual decision; distinguish your interpretation from a factual claim about the scene.

What are the limits of this exercise?

Never publish identifying faces or location metadata without proper permission. A contact sheet does not certify authenticity.
