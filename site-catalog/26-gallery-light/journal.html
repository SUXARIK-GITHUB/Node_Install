Example and analysis

One object under three lighting conditions

Set a harmless object on a matte surface, then observe it near a window, under a diffused lamp and under a small directional light. Keep the viewpoint fixed. Describe the shape and hardness of the shadows before deciding which setup communicates the object best.

A glossy surface may reveal the reflection of a light source while a textured matte surface spreads highlights. Instead of saying the second surface is darker, compare where reflections appear and what details remain legible. Move only one light at a time to learn which change caused which effect.

Try three crops of the same scene: close detail, balanced view and wider context. The extra empty space may change the implied scale. Record these as visual studies, not measurements or photographs of an actual exhibition.

Daylight studies

The same surface changes character as daylight moves across it.

A white surface changes from cool to warm as daylight moves through a room. Take comparable photographs with identical exposure to separate lighting from material color.

Photograph one blank surface from the same position at morning and late-afternoon light, recording when the edge of a shadow changes character.

02 / Art

Gentle shadows

Soft light makes gradual shadows while a small source draws sharp edges.

A larger source produces gentler transitions between light and shade. Moving the light closer also changes the relative size and apparent softness.

Place a diffuse shade and a small directed lamp near a harmless object, then sketch the resulting transitions. Keep exposure and object position consistent.

03 / Art

Surface detail

Gloss and roughness reflect the room in very different ways.

Gloss reflects details of the environment while matte surfaces scatter light. Look for window shapes in reflections before deciding that an object itself is uneven.

Compare a glossy and a matte sample under the same light and describe reflected shapes rather than calling one material simply brighter or darker.

04 / Art

Framing a room

A frame should leave enough space for the eye to notice silence.

An object needs some empty space around its silhouette to read clearly. Vary the distance to the border and compare what first attracts the eye.

Draw three possible crops of a quiet interior and mark where the eye rests in each. Leave room for light and empty space instead of filling every corner.

What to look for: One object under three lighting conditions

Move one light source while keeping the object and viewpoint fixed. A highlight may travel across a glossy surface while a matte surface changes more gently; that difference says something about appearance, not the material's precise composition. Use three controlled frames to separate the effect of lighting from the effect of cropping.
