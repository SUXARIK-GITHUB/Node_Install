Line length

Line length affects the effort required to find the next line.

Line length affects the movement from the end of one line to the start of the next. Try a narrow column and a wide one with the same article.

Set one paragraph at three line lengths, keeping the same words and typeface. Compare the ease of returning to the next line on a phone and on paper.

Hierarchy and scale

Hierarchy should remain understandable without color.

Hierarchy is easier to follow when heading sizes and spacing use a limited scale. Check the result on small screens and with longer translations.

Build a heading scale for a short tutorial, then test whether two adjacent levels are distinguishable without relying on color alone.

03 / Learning

Pairing type

Two typefaces are often enough when their roles are clear.

Pairing typefaces requires different roles, not simply two visually interesting fonts. Compare a calm body face with a clear display headline.

Pair two accessible typefaces in a sample article and label the role of each. Avoid pairing solely because their names belong to different categories.

Reading on mobile

On mobile screens readability depends on spacing as well as size.

Mobile reading benefits from generous line spacing and predictable navigation. Test actual text at increased browser zoom instead of judging a scaled screenshot.

Inspect a reading page at narrow widths and enlarged text, checking long words, links and focus indicators. Record where the layout begins to fail.

What to look for: A paragraph study across two line lengths

Readable typography emerges from relationships among line length, spacing, contrast and the language being set. Test ordinary body text at different viewport widths and with larger system type. A headline that looks striking in a mockup is not enough if captions, errors and navigation become difficult to understand.

Reading questions

  1. While reading “Line length”, look for observable evidence related to “Measure”. Line length recorded consistently. Compare it with “Hierarchy and scale”: which condition needs its own note before a conclusion is justified? What is directly visible, and what remains an interpretation?

  2. While reading “Hierarchy and scale”, look for observable evidence related to “Rhythm”. Spacing and punctuation remain legible. Compare it with “Pairing type”: which condition needs its own note before a conclusion is justified? What is directly visible, and what remains an interpretation?

  3. While reading “Pairing type”, look for observable evidence related to “Reflow”. No clipped text at high zoom. Compare it with “Reading on mobile”: which condition needs its own note before a conclusion is justified? What is directly visible, and what remains an interpretation?

Field guide

A paragraph study across two line lengths

Type design needs to be judged on running text, not just striking headlines. This exercise uses the same sample on two page widths.

Method

  1. Write a short original passage with punctuation, digits and a long word so the layout must handle varied characters.
  2. Set it twice with the same font and size while adjusting only the text measure and line spacing.
  3. Check ragged edges, broken words, keyboard navigation and reflow at 200% browser zoom.

Quick reference

FeatureWhat to check
MeasureLine length recorded consistently.
RhythmSpacing and punctuation remain legible.
ReflowNo clipped text at high zoom.

Limits and safety

Typography preferences depend on reader, language and device. A sample cannot certify accessibility by itself.
