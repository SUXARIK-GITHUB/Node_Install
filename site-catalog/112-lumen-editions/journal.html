The same shape becomes another object when it changes size, weight, or position.

01

Folding light

A study in three translucent planes: each layer reveals another edge. The composition uses only vectors and can be reproduced without external imagery.

Layering works when each plane has a distinct edge and transparency. Print a small test if the effect is meant for paper rather than a screen.

02

The wide margin

Whitespace gives each shape time to register. The next mark matters because the page leaves space for it.

A wide margin is not unused space: it controls where attention settles. Compare one balanced page with an intentionally crowded alternate.

03

Cut paper

Overlapping fields suggest material thickness without imitating any particular real work or artist.

Overlapping paper forms cast small shadows that suggest depth. Observe the real material before simplifying the effect into a flat vector.

04

An unfinished square

A broken frame is still legible; perception fills the missing corner. The exercise explores how little a form needs.

The mind completes interrupted contours when enough of the geometry remains. Remove one segment at a time until the object stops reading clearly.

What to look for: One geometric composition, three arrangements

In a paper composition, the space left around a shape can be as expressive as the shape itself. Compare three arrangements while changing only overlap or scale. These original geometric studies can discuss rhythm and balance without attributing the result to a nonexistent gallery exhibition or physical edition.

Field guide

One geometric composition, three arrangements

A compact exercise in visual hierarchy needs only two rectangles and one circle. The forms are intentionally abstract: their spacing, order and overlap are the material of the study.

Method

  1. Draw a large pale rectangle, a smaller dark rectangle and a circle. In the first sketch use a centered balance with generous white space on every side.
  2. In the second, move the circle near an edge without clipping it. Keep colors and sizes unchanged. Notice how the sense of motion changes even when the objects are identical.
  3. In the third, overlap the smaller rectangle with the circle. Compare the edges that remain visible and write down which form a new viewer notices first.

Limits and safety

These are proposed exercises, not measurements from a real exhibition or claims about a particular artist. Results depend on scale and presentation.
