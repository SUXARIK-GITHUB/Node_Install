Four paths for further study

Choose the subject most relevant to your question. Each item leads to an article and a practical exercise.

  1. Color relationships

    Make a swatch board with one dominant hue, one supporting tone and a neutral area. Compare the same relationship on paper and a bright screen.

  2. A useful grid

    Lay out six unrelated objects on a grid, then change only the spacing and alignment. Note which grouping becomes easier to read without altering the content.

  3. Prototype studies

    Cut a small paper prototype of one everyday object, photograph it from two viewpoints and record the practical issue each view reveals.

  4. Small exhibitions

    Plan a miniature display around three objects with a clear entry point and enough breathing room. Label material and scale instead of inventing an exhibition history.

Method and limits

The following is an independent reading exercise, not a report of fieldwork.

A visual series is easier to evaluate when only the framing changes. This exercise uses one ordinary object and avoids invented campaigns or client work.

Arrange the frames in a row and ask which communicates context, material and detail; note that preference is not a measured performance result.

These are illustrative compositions, not commissioned design work. Check rights before publishing third-party artwork or photographs.
