Questions

Answers on reading a raster, cloud coverage and related reading from Signal Atlas.

Reading a raster: What does one raster pixel represent?

A raster pixel represents an area rather than a photographic point.

Cloud coverage: How does cloud cover affect interpretation?

Clouds, haze and their shadows can resemble a change on the ground. Compare acquisitions from nearby dates before treating a missing feature as a land-use event.

Ground sampling: Which details can ground sampling resolve?

Place a small road and a large lake on sketches with different sampling distances. Decide which shape remains interpretable at each scale without fabricating detail.

What should I check in this material: First observation?

A raster cell represents a ground footprint. A picture displayed at two hundred percent zoom still contains the same source measurements; it does not gain new streets or tree crowns. What should I check in this material: Second observation?

Cloud cover, shadow and viewing angle can all prevent a clear comparison between two dates. Mask unknown areas instead of explaining them as real landscape change. Record the acquisition dates, coordinate system and processing steps with the map, so the limitations are visible. What should I check in this material: Further reading?

For a classroom exercise, sketch a coastline on squared paper, then redraw it using larger cells. Notice which narrow features disappear. This illustrates resolution loss without claiming that the drawing is an actual satellite observation. What are the limits of this exercise?

A satellite image is an observation tied to time, resolution and a particular sensor. Brightness alone rarely establishes what a surface contains. Compare two scenes only after checking cloud cover, spatial scale and coordinate reference; a difference in color may reflect the imaging method rather than a real change on the ground.
