Four paths for further study

Choose the subject most relevant to your question. Each item leads to an article and a practical exercise.

  1. A quiet scale

    Design a short practice sequence for one simple scale with a slow tempo and comfortable range. Avoid assigning a fixed speed as a measure of progress.

  2. Listening to resonance

    Listen to the decay of a single original piano note at different dynamics and describe what you hear. Room acoustics can change the impression.

  3. Hands and posture

    Sketch neutral wrist and hand positions at the keyboard, leaving room for individual anatomy. Persistent pain is a reason to stop and seek guidance.

  4. A patient practice

    Keep a practice log with duration, one technical focus and the next small question. Consistent notes can be more useful than a long unstructured session.

Method and limits

The following is an independent reading exercise, not a report of fieldwork.

A practice diary is more useful than an unsupported claim of progress. This exercise documents one self-chosen musical phrase.

Repeat on another day and compare descriptions of the same difficulty without assigning invented accuracy percentages.

Take breaks if discomfort appears; this page does not reproduce copyrighted sheet music or replace a music teacher.
