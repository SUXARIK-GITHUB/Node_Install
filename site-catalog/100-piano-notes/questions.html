Questions

Answers on a quiet scale, listening to resonance and related reading from Piano Room.

A quiet scale: How can a simple scale build practice?

A quiet scale teaches more when each note is heard distinctly.

Listening to resonance: What should a listener notice in resonance?

Resonance changes when notes overlap and the damper pedal is used. Listen to how harmony clears when the pedal is released.

Hands and posture: How should hands be positioned comfortably?

Sketch neutral wrist and hand positions at the keyboard, leaving room for individual anatomy. Persistent pain is a reason to stop and seek guidance.

What should I check in this material: Fingering?

Positions are comfortable and repeatable. Example: Select an original or public-domain four-measure phrase and mark the fingering you intend to try at a comfortable tempo.

What should I check in this material: Rhythm?

Uneven transitions are identified. Example: Play slowly with relaxed hands, noting only where rhythm or transitions become uneven instead of forcing speed.

What should I check in this material: Review?

Later notes refer to the same phrase. Example: Repeat on another day and compare descriptions of the same difficulty without assigning invented accuracy percentages.

What are the limits of this exercise?

Take breaks if discomfort appears; this page does not reproduce copyrighted sheet music or replace a music teacher.
