No sign-in, external scripts or third-party analytics.

Four paths for further study

Choose the subject most relevant to your question. Each item leads to an article and a practical exercise.

  1. Bold hierarchy

    Make three typographic treatments of one original headline using only size, weight and spacing. Compare recognition from across a room and on a phone.

  2. A two-color poster

    Construct a poster with two colors and a neutral paper field, checking text contrast rather than assuming a bright accent stays readable.

  3. Letter spacing

    Print a few lines with intentionally tight and loose tracking, then note where individual letters begin to merge. Keep the source text identical.

  4. Printed texture

    Sketch the effect of rough paper and misregistration on a small printed mark. Avoid presenting an on-screen filter as a real print test.

Method and limits

The following is an independent reading exercise, not a report of fieldwork.

A poster can be legible without relying on decoration. An exercise with one headline, one fact and a date reveals the effect of scale.

Test both at high zoom and in monochrome so the information remains understandable without color cues.

Do not present a sample event as a real public announcement. Respect image and font licensing when producing a finished poster.
